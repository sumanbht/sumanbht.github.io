Research

I am an observational astrophysicist whose research focuses on time-domain stellar astrophysics, stellar variability, and emission-line stars. My work investigates the evolution of circumstellar disks, binary interactions, and transient phenomena in Classical Be stars using large multi-epoch datasets from ground- and space-based observatories such as ESO, TESS, Gaia, LAMOST, and BeSS.

I combine spectroscopy, photometry, machine learning, and statistical analysis to explore stellar evolution. My methodological approach includes advanced statistical data modeling and interpolation techniques—such as integrating Piecewise Cubic Hermite Interpolating Polynomials (PCHIP) with Gaussian Process (GP) regression—to analyze complex binned spectral data. I specifically focus on analyzing the iron emission lines of Be stars within the 6000 to 8000 angstrom range to advance our understanding of transient astrophysical phenomena.

Observing Proposals

I have been successful as a PI and Co-PI on numerous observing proposals, securing substantial telescope time across major observatories:

  • XSHOOTER (VLT UT3): Exploring the Transition Phase of Rapidly Rotating Massive Stars (Proposal cycle: P115, PI).
  • NASA-IRTF (SpeX): Infrared line analysis of Transition Phase candidates (15.5 hours across two cycles, PI).
  • Vainu Bappu Observatory (1-m & 1.02-m telescopes): Investigating the transient nature of classical Be stars (78 nights total, Co-PI).
  • Himalayan Chandra Telescope (HFOSC): Spectroscopic monitoring program of classical Be stars (Cycles C1 & C2, Co-PI).
  • VBT Echelle Spectrograph: Spectroscopic survey of emission-line stars and K-HiReSS survey (Over 25 nights, PI/Co-PI).
  • J.C. Bhattacharyya Telescope (UKATC): Photometric study of the BeXRB system HD 54786 (8 nights, PI).
